# Subject-Matter-Mathematics

### Mathematics Lesson Design • Curriculum Development • Assessment & Misconception Analysis

A portfolio of **K–12 Mathematics instructional designs** demonstrating how mathematical concepts can be transformed into structured learning experiences—from identifying learning needs and misconceptions to designing instruction and creating scaffolded practice.

This repository showcases my work as a **Mathematics Subject Matter Expert (SME)** with 7+ years of experience in mathematics teaching, assessment design, curriculum development, and academic leadership.

---

## 🎯 Purpose

The goal of this repository is to demonstrate the complete instructional design process for Mathematics:

**Concept → Learning Objectives → Misconception Analysis → Lesson Design → Lesson Plan → Practice → Assessment**

The projects focus not only on mathematical accuracy, but also on how learners develop understanding, where they are likely to struggle, and how instructional materials can address those challenges.

## 🧠 Design Philosophy

### 1. Misconception-First Design

Lessons consider **how learners are likely to misunderstand a concept**, rather than focusing only on presenting the correct solution.

This helps ensure that explanations, examples, questions, and practice activities directly address learner difficulties.

---

### 2. Concept Before Procedure

Where appropriate, the materials prioritize conceptual understanding before introducing formal procedures or formulas.

For example, quadratic relationships can first be explored through **patterns and differences** before moving toward formal algebraic representation.

---

### 3. Scaffolded Learning

Learning activities are structured to gradually reduce support:

**Worked Example → Guided Practice → Supported Practice → Independent Application**

This allows learners to build understanding and confidence before solving problems independently.

---

### 4. Visual and Mathematical Representation

Several projects deliberately connect different representations of the same mathematical idea:

**Visual → Numerical → Algebraic → Applied**

This helps learners develop flexible mathematical thinking rather than memorizing isolated procedures.

---

### 5. Alignment Between Instruction and Assessment

Learning objectives, activities, questions, and practice tasks are designed around the same intended learning outcomes.

The objective is to maintain alignment between:

**What learners should understand → What they are taught → What they practise → What they are assessed on**
